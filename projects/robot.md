# ASME Student Design Competition – Competitive Robot Design

**Organization:** ASME Student Design Competition (SDC)  
**Competition Theme:** Robot Mini Golf (2023–2024)  
**Role:** Drivetrain Mechanical Design Team Member  
**Focus Areas:** Mechanical design, electromechanical integration, design under constraints

---

## Project Overview

The ASME Student Design Competition (SDC) challenges undergraduate engineering teams to **design, fabricate, and operate a remotely controlled robot** capable of completing a nine-obstacle mini-golf course within strict size, power, and operational constraints.

The competition emphasizes:
- Proper engineering design practices
- Fabrication and testing discipline
- Performance under time and rule constraints
- Creativity within a well-defined problem space

Our team designed and built a compact robotic system to navigate the course, manipulate a golf ball, and complete obstacles reliably within the competition rules.

---

## Engineering Problem

The robot was required to:

- Fit entirely within a **50 cm × 50 cm × 50 cm rigid sizing box** at the start of each run
- Operate using **rechargeable battery power**
- Navigate a complex mini-golf field featuring tunnels, ramps, bridges, hoops, and moving obstacles
- Perform controlled “putts” and ball manipulation without bulldozing or transferring momentum illegally
- Complete as many obstacles as possible within a **10-minute time limit**

Design validation, testing discipline, and time management were explicitly rewarded in competition scoring

---

## Technical Approach

### Mechanical Design
- Contributed to mechanical layout and packaging to satisfy strict **sizing box constraints**
- Supported design of mechanisms for controlled ball interaction and obstacle traversal
- Considered expandability after deployment while remaining within maximum operational dimensions

### System Integration
- Integrated mechanical components with motors, power systems, and remote control hardware
- Balanced compactness, stability, and maneuverability across varied obstacle geometries
- Designed with robustness in mind to withstand repeated runs and field interactions

### Design Constraints
- Strict adherence to competition rules governing:
  - Device size and expansion
  - Battery usage and replacement
  - Ball contact legality
  - Field interaction and safety

Engineering decisions were driven by **rule interpretation, risk mitigation, and repeatability**, not just theoretical performance.

---

## Testing & Iteration

- Conducted iterative testing to evaluate:
  - Maneuverability through tunnels and narrow passages
  - Controlled stopping and putting behavior
  - Reliability across multiple obstacle types
  - Refined mechanical features based on observed failure modes and rule compliance

---

## Outcomes & Learning

This project reinforced several core engineering principles:

- Designing within **tight physical and operational constraints**
- Translating written rules into concrete engineering requirements
- Balancing mechanical simplicity with functional capability
- Appreciating the role of testing, validation, and time management in competitive engineering environments

The experience represents a meaningful application of **mechanical design, systems thinking, and team-based engineering**.
