# Tensile Testing of Metals – Stress–Strain Behavior

**Course:** MIE 302 – Mechanical Engineering Laboratory I  
**Project Type:** Materials Characterization Experiment  
**Timeline:** Spring 2026  
**Focus Areas:** Tensile testing, stress–strain analysis, mechanical properties of metals

---

## Project Overview

This laboratory experiment investigated the **stress–strain behavior of common engineering metals** through uniaxial tensile testing.

Material specimens were loaded to failure using an **Instron universal testing machine**, allowing characterization of key mechanical properties including:

- Elastic modulus
- Yield strength
- Ultimate tensile strength
- Fracture strength
- Ductility

The experiment also explored relationships between **hardness and strength** as well as correlations between **strength and ductility** across different metals.

---

## Engineering Objective

The goal of the experiment was to understand how metals respond to tensile loading and to characterize their mechanical behavior using **stress–strain curves**.

Specific objectives included:

- Measuring **engineering stress–strain behavior** during tensile testing
- Determining fundamental material properties
- Comparing mechanical performance across multiple metals
- Investigating relationships between hardness, strength, and ductility

Understanding these relationships is essential in selecting materials for **structural and mechanical applications**.

---

## Materials Tested

Three common engineering metals were evaluated:

- **1010 Steel (normalized at 900°C)**
- **Alloy 260 Cartridge Brass**
- **6061-T6 Aluminum**

These materials represent different classes of structural metals with varying combinations of strength and ductility.

---

## Experimental Equipment

Testing was conducted using:

- **Instron 34SC-5 Universal Testing Machine**
- **Extensometer** for strain measurement
- **Digital calipers and micrometer** for specimen dimension measurements
- **Rockwell hardness tester**

These instruments allowed precise measurement of applied force, specimen deformation, and material hardness.

---

## Experimental Method

### Specimen Preparation

Before testing, each specimen’s dimensions were carefully measured using calipers and micrometers to determine the **initial cross-sectional area** required for stress calculations.

Hardness measurements were also taken in the specimen grip regions to determine initial material hardness.

---

### Tensile Testing Procedure

Each specimen was mounted in the Instron testing machine and loaded in tension until fracture.

During testing, the system recorded:

- Applied load
- Extension
- Strain data from the extensometer

These measurements were used to construct **engineering stress–strain curves**.

---

### Mechanical Properties Determined

From the stress–strain curves, the following properties were extracted:

- **Elastic Modulus (E)**
- **Yield Strength (YS)**
- **Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS)**
- **Fracture Strength (FS)**
- **Strain to Failure (StF)**
- **Ductility**

These parameters describe how materials behave under load and how they fail.

---

## Data Analysis

The experiment included several analysis components:

- Construction of **engineering stress–strain curves**
- Conversion to **true stress–true strain curves**
- Calculation of key mechanical properties
- Comparison of material behavior across metals

Additional analysis explored correlations between:

- **Hardness vs. yield strength**
- **Hardness vs. ultimate tensile strength**
- **Ductility vs. strength**

These relationships help illustrate how mechanical properties interact in real engineering materials.

---

## Preliminary Observations

Initial testing demonstrated the expected mechanical behavior differences among the metals.

Typical trends observed in tensile testing include:

- Steel exhibiting **high strength with moderate ductility**
- Aluminum showing **moderate strength with good ductility**
- Brass demonstrating **high ductility and lower yield strength**
