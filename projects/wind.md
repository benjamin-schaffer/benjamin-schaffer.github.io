## Project Overview

The Collegiate Wind Competition (CWC) is a national workforce-development competition that challenges undergraduate teams to solve real wind-energy engineering problems through two parallel tracks:

1) **Turbine Design & Testing Contest** – design/build a wind-driven power system and test it in a wind tunnel  
2) **Project Development & Engagement Contest** – develop a hypothetical wind farm plan and conduct outreach/industry engagement  

Teams are expected to compete in both contests and present to technical reviewers at the regional event.

## Engineering Constraints (Rules-Driven Design)

CWC design is constraint-heavy and evaluated through inspection + objective test tasks.

### Wind Tunnel Envelope + Interfaces
The turbine must fit within a defined volume in the tunnel:
- Rotor + non-rotor components within a **45 × 45 × 45 cm cube**
- Non-rotor components constrained within a **15.8 cm diameter cylinder** below the cube
- Rotor midplane height must be within a target band relative to tunnel centerline  
These geometry constraints directly drive tower/nacelle packaging, mounting, and serviceability.

### Electrical + Safety Requirements
Key electrical constraints include:
- **DC voltage at the point of common coupling (PCC) must be ≤ 48 V at all times**
- Teams interface to the PCC using **Anderson Powerpole (PP15-45) connectors** with correct polarity
- Turbines must support **shutdown on command (emergency stop)** and also shutdown when disconnected from the load  
The rules strongly reward designs that are robust, safe, and unambiguous during inspection and testing.

### Inspection + Timed Installation
Before testing, teams must pass technical and safety inspection and demonstrate a repeatable installation/commissioning approach. The event includes a **timed install + commissioning period**, so practical assembly sequence and connectorization matter—not just performance.

## Testing & Scoring (What We Design Toward)

CWC turbine performance testing includes multiple tasks that incentivize durability and controllability, not just peak power:
- **Power curve performance** across multiple wind speeds
- **Control of rated power** at higher winds (keeping power consistent vs 10 m/s baseline)
- **Safety task** (shutdown + restart behavior)
- **Durability task** (survive and stay online under higher wind)

These tasks shape our design priorities: repeatability, safe shutdown logic, mechanical robustness, and stable electrical output.
